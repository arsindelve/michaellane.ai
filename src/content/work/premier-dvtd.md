## The moment

About a hundred people in the room. More watching on the live stream. Our VP of Marketing walked them through the new company, the new brands, the new mission, and then got to the part nobody in the audience could see coming.

The point.

Across the room, straight at me.

I flipped it. One switch, after seven months of work by six people: a new storefront, a new back office, a new portal for ten thousand sellers, ten servers warmed up and waiting.

Thumbs up.

And we were live. Just like that.

No spinner, no apology, no "give us a minute." The old site was gone and the new business was open, in front of everyone, on the day we'd circled in January. That's the whole job, really: make the hardest moment of the project look like nothing happened.

## From jewelry parties to a marketplace with a mission

Since 1985, Premier Designs had sold jewelry through about ten thousand independent Jewelers and home parties. DVTD was a different business entirely: an online marketplace where shoppers bought from many mission-driven brands, and every purchase gave back to causes like anti-trafficking, hunger relief and job creation. New products, new partners, new rewards, and a new relationship between sellers and customers.

That meant nothing about the old platform survived. We replaced it all:

- **Retired** a homegrown system running on Oracle.
- **Built** the new storefront on Episerver, with .NET and Angular.
- **Integrated** it with Dynamics 365 as the business system behind it.
- **Created** a completely bespoke portal for the Jewelers, so ten thousand sellers could run their business in the new model from day one.

## Six people

It was my team of six, and I was the VP. On this project I was also the product owner and the project manager, so the backlog, the schedule and the scope trade-offs were mine too. I did all of the Azure setup for the cutover myself.

## How we made the date

A date announced on stage doesn't slip, so we planned for failure instead of hoping against it:

- **A pre-mortem:** we imagined the launch had gone wrong and worked backwards to every way it could.
- **A runbook** for the cutover, step by step, with owners and rollback points.
- **A full rehearsal:** we played the launch through end to end before the real one.
- **Capacity:** we scaled out to ten virtual servers for the reveal, then back down.

On the day, it was boring. That was the goal.

## Afterwards

A few months later, the company closed. The business model didn't survive 2020. The launch did exactly what we planned, on the day we planned it in January, and I'm still proud of it.

I can ship. I can hit dates. I can execute.
