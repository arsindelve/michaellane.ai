## The napkin

LinkGreen was founded in 2013 by Warren Patterson and Rob Tigwell. Their idea was simple and overdue: wholesale ordering in the horticulture industry still ran on phone calls, faxes and printed catalogues, and every order a garden centre placed by hand was slow, expensive and easy to get wrong. LinkGreen would connect suppliers and buyers on one neutral platform, so every supplier could reach all their buyers and every buyer could order from all their suppliers.

When I arrived, there was a napkin diagram and no code. I wrote the first line.

## Eight months

Eight months later it was live, with real suppliers and real buyers placing real orders. I built the platform on ASP.NET MVC and Angular over SQL Server, with NServiceBus on Azure Service Bus to move orders reliably between parties, and set it up in Azure with automated tests and daily deployments from the start.

I also built the mobile app, with Ionic, so one codebase shipped to both iPhone and Android. It wasn't a cut-down companion app: it did everything the website did, so a buyer could build an order walking the sales floor and a supplier's rep could take one on the road.

## From one engineer to a team

At first it was just me. As LinkGreen grew from a niche in Ontario to customers across North America, I hired three developers and grew into the CTO role, scaling the engineering as the company scaled.

## Still going

LinkGreen grew beyond horticulture into hardware, building materials, food and more, and today it's OrderEase, a B2B order platform used by companies like Scotts Canada and Milwaukee Tools. I'm proud that it started with a line of code I wrote, sitting next to a napkin.
