## The product

The Logitech Revue was the first Google TV box: a living-room computer that put the web, search and apps on your television, years before that was normal. It came with a full keyboard remote, and phones could control it too.

## My part

I managed the local and offshore engineering teams, ran them as their Scrum Master, and brought in automated acceptance testing, continuous integration and test-driven design. I worked directly with Google to create the APIs for the Revue's remote control, the layer that let a keyboard, a remote and a phone all drive the same box. It was my first management role, and I learned what it takes to ship hardware and software with a partner the size of Google on a fixed launch date.

## How it went

Commercially? Badly. In November 2011, Logitech's CEO called the Revue a "gigantic" mistake that had cost the company more than $100 million. Most of the press agreed.

In fairness to the box, it wanted you to search, browse and install apps on your TV with a remote in 2010. Today that's every TV sold. Being early is indistinguishable from being wrong, right up until it isn't.

The engineering was incredible, and I'd do it again. I still loved being on the team that built it.
